Jesus of Nazareth: The Humble King Who Split Human History in Two

Updated 2026-11-09 • By PB Ideas Virtuales Team

Heartwarming holiday story: Jesus of Nazareth: The Humble King Who Split Human History in Two.

Over two thousand years ago, in an obscure province on the eastern frontier of the Roman Empire, an imperial tax decree set a quiet carpenter and his betrothed on a dusty road toward Bethlehem of Judea. Caesar Augustus ruled from marble palaces in Rome, unaware that the child about to be born in a humble feeding trough for animals would outlast his empire and divide the very calendar of human civilization into before and after.

Our primary historical windows into the Nativity are the Gospels of Matthew and Luke, written in the first century, alongside brief references to the life of Jesus by non-Christian Roman and Jewish historians such as Tacitus and Flavius Josephus. Luke records that Mary wrapped her newborn son in swaddling cloths and laid Him in a manger because there was no room for them in the lodging place, while ordinary shepherds keeping night watch in nearby fields became the first witnesses of the birth.

Although our modern calendar counts the years from Christ's birth, historians place the actual Nativity between 6 and 4 BC, shortly before the death of King Herod the Great. The discrepancy arose in the sixth century when the monk Dionysius Exiguus calculated the calendar system of Anno Domini and misdated Herod's reign by a few years. Early Christians did not initially record the exact calendar day of the birth; it was in the fourth century, as documented in the Roman Chronograph of 354, that the Church formally fixed the celebration on December 25.

Tradition from the second century onward—preserved by early Christian writers such as Justin Martyr—describes the birthplace in Bethlehem not as a wooden cabin of European winter art, but as a limestone cave carved into the Judean hills, where families sheltered their livestock beneath their homes.

From that silent stone hollow, without armies, wealth, or earthly crowns, the life and teachings of Jesus of Nazareth reshaped human morality, inspired hospitals, universities, and sacred art, and gave the world the enduring heartbeat of Christmas.

Christmas Reflection: As the Gospel of John proclaims, 'The Word became flesh and dwelt among us' (John 1:14). The supreme mystery of Christmas is that Almighty God did not approach humanity through intimidating power, but through the vulnerability of a newborn child. By choosing a humble manger in Bethlehem, our Lord Jesus Christ, the true Light of the World, showed that no human heart is too broken, poor, or forgotten to become His dwelling place of eternal love and peace.
