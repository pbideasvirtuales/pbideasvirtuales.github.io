George Frideric Handel: The 24 Days of Heavenly Ecstasy That Birthed the 'Messiah'

Updated 2026-11-09 • By PB Ideas Virtuales Team

Heartwarming holiday story: George Frideric Handel: The 24 Days of Heavenly Ecstasy That Birthed the 'Messiah'.

In the summer of 1741, fifty-six-year-old George Frideric Handel walked the streets of London weighed down by financial exhaustion, declining box-office returns for his Italian operas, and the lingering effects of a stroke that had temporarily paralyzed his right arm four years earlier. Many London critics quietly assumed the German-born composer's greatest days were behind him, unaware that he was about to embark on the most astonishing creative sprint in musical history.

Everything changed when Handel's friend and collaborator Charles Jennens sent him a new libretto compiled entirely from the King James Bible and the Book of Common Prayer, tracing the prophecies of Isaiah, the birth and Passion of Christ, and the promise of resurrection. Locking himself inside his brick townhouse at 25 Brook Street on August 22, 1741, Handel began setting the sacred text to music.

The original autograph manuscript preserved today in the British Library confirms the dates in Handel's own hand: he completed Part I—containing the radiant Christmas prophecies—in just six days, Part II in nine days, and Part III in six days, before filling in the orchestration to finish the entire 259-page score of 'Messiah' on September 14, 1741, after precisely twenty-four days. At the bottom of the final page, he inscribed the letters 'SDG'—Soli Deo Gloria, 'To God alone be the glory.'

A famous account recorded decades later claims that Handel's servant found him weeping over the 'Hallelujah' chorus, exclaiming, 'I did think I did see all Heaven before me, and the great God Himself.' While historians note that this moving quote cannot be verified in contemporary 1741 documents, the sheer speed and exaltation of his manuscript testify to an extraordinary outpouring of inspiration.

Premiered in Dublin on April 13, 1742, to raise funds for prisoners' debt relief and local hospitals, 'Messiah' freed 142 indebted prisoners on its first performance and became an annual benefit tradition that has lifted humanity's eyes toward heaven ever since.

Christmas Reflection: Handel's twenty-four days in that quiet London room remind us that our darkest seasons of exhaustion and trial are often the very threshold of grace. When we lift our eyes beyond our personal struggles to contemplate our Lord Jesus Christ—whom Isaiah called 'Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace' (Isaiah 9:6)—He restores our strength and turns our deepest weariness into an eternal 'Hallelujah' of gratitude and service.
